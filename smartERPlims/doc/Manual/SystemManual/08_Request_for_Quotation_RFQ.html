Request for Quotation (RFQ)

Staff procedure and online supplier quotation guide

← Back to System Manual

1. What the RFQ module does

The Request for Quotation (RFQ) module allows procurement staff to invite selected suppliers to quote for required goods or services. Suppliers complete and submit their quotations online through a secure invitation link. Staff then review and compare the submitted quotations, select the successful quotation and proceed to create a purchase order.

Important: The supplier completes the quotation online. Staff do not need to enter the supplier's prices on the supplier's behalf.

3. Staff: create and issue an RFQ

  1. Open Purchases → Transactions → Request for Quotation (RFQ).
  2. Create an RFQ and enter its title, currency and quotation closing date/time.
  3. Enter the purpose or description of the request.
  4. Add each requested product or service. Provide a description, technical specification or grade, pack size where applicable, quantity and unit. An inventory item may be linked when appropriate; free-text descriptions are also supported.
  5. Enter the RFQ terms and conditions. Include relevant technical, quality, delivery, payment, packaging, shelf-life, certification, safety-data-sheet and return requirements.
  6. Save the RFQ as a draft.
  7. Use the supplier invitation section for that draft and select the suppliers to invite. Check that each supplier has a valid email address in the supplier master.
  8. Select Issue RFQ and email invitations. The system creates an individual invitation token and sends each selected supplier an email.

3.1 Understanding the RFQ terms and conditions

The Terms and conditions field records the purchaser's requirements and conditions that suppliers must consider when preparing their quotations. It is not the supplier's quotation or a place to enter the supplier's proposed terms. These requirements help all invited suppliers quote on a consistent basis and help procurement assess whether an offer is technically and commercially acceptable.

Purchaser sets the actual conditions: Examples such as 12 months' remaining shelf life, a particular delivery deadline or a specific payment period are illustrative only. Enter the requirements that apply to the purchase. Do not assume example values are mandatory for every RFQ.

Example: RFQ terms for laboratory reagents

The following is a sample that procurement can adapt. Remove or amend any condition that does not apply to the goods being requested.

  • Specification: Supply the reagent in the grade, concentration, pack size and manufacturer specification stated against each RFQ line. Identify the manufacturer and product/catalogue number in the quotation.
  • Quality and documentation: Supply new, genuine products with batch/lot numbers and expiry dates. Provide a Certificate of Analysis and the applicable Safety Data Sheet (SDS) with the goods.
  • Packaging: Deliver in original, unopened and properly labelled manufacturer packaging. Packaging must protect the contents during transport and storage.
  • Shelf life: State the expiry date and confirm that the goods will meet the minimum remaining shelf-life requirement specified by the purchaser at delivery.
  • Delivery: State the delivery lead time and any delivery charges. Deliver to the location specified in the RFQ or subsequent purchase order.
  • Pricing and tax: Quote in the currency stated in the RFQ. Clearly indicate whether prices include or exclude applicable taxes and identify any additional charges.
  • Payment: State the payment terms requested by the supplier and note that the final agreed terms are subject to the purchaser's approval and purchase order.
  • Non-conforming goods: Incorrect, damaged, expired or non-conforming goods may be rejected and must be collected and replaced by the supplier under the agreed return/replacement terms.
  • Quotation validity: State the period for which the quotation and prices remain valid.

Supplier responses, exceptions and deviations

Suppliers should quote against the purchaser's stated requirements. If a supplier cannot meet a requirement or is proposing an alternative, the supplier must clearly disclose the exception in the quotation notes/deviations field. Examples include a different manufacturer, an alternative grade or pack size, a shorter shelf life, additional delivery charges or different payment terms.

Procurement should review each disclosed deviation before comparing or awarding quotations. A supplier's alternative condition must not be treated as accepted merely because it appears in the quotation; acceptance is subject to the organisation's evaluation and approval process.

4. What the supplier receives

The invitation is sent to the supplier's registered email address. Its subject is Request for Quotation [RFQ number]. The sender display name is taken from the company name configured in the ERP.

The message identifies the supplier, RFQ title and RFQ reference. It asks the supplier to review the requirements and submit before the deadline, and provides the link labelled Open RFQ and submit quotation.

The link opens SupplierRFQ.php. Its URL contains the company/database identifier and a unique token for that supplier. The token is validated by the portal before the RFQ form is displayed.

Invitation security: The link is supplier-specific. Do not publish it or forward it to another supplier. If the invitation is sent to the wrong email address, correct the supplier contact details and handle the invitation through the procurement administrator.

5. Supplier: submit a quotation online

  1. Open the invitation email and select Open RFQ and submit quotation.
  2. Review the RFQ reference, requested items, quantities, specifications and terms displayed in the supplier portal.
  3. Enter the requested unit prices and applicable tax information for the quotation lines.
  4. Indicate whether quoted prices are tax-inclusive, as applicable.
  5. Complete the commercial details requested by the form, including availability, delivery information or delivery lead time, quotation reference, quotation validity, payment terms and notes.
  6. Check all prices, taxes, quantities and terms carefully, then submit the quotation.

The supplier portal is separate from the staff ERP session. The supplier does not need a staff LIMS/ERP username or password; access is granted through the invitation token.

6. One final submission

An invitation is intended for one final quotation submission. The system validates the invitation token and prevents the same invitation from being used to submit another quotation. Suppliers should therefore verify their entries before submitting.

7. Staff: compare, award and create the purchase order

  1. Return to the RFQ page and open the quotation comparison for the relevant RFQ.
  2. Review the quotations received from invited suppliers, including prices and the commercial details supplied.
  3. Select the quotation to award. Ensure the selection is supported by the organisation's procurement and approval procedures; the lowest price is not automatically the best-value decision.
  4. After the award is recorded, use Create Purchase Order from awarded quotation to proceed to purchase-order creation.
  5. For conversion, each RFQ line must be mapped to an active inventory item and a matching unit in the stock master. Resolve unmapped items or unit differences before conversion.
  6. Complete the normal purchase-order checks and approval process.
PO conversion constraint: The current integration does not infer pack-size or unit conversions. Where an RFQ line cannot be matched safely to an active stock item and its unit, correct the mapping before creating the PO.

8. Email and deployment checks

  • Confirm the ERP email configuration and SMTP credentials are valid.
  • Confirm the application is accessed through HTTPS so supplier invitation links use the secure site address.
  • Confirm the RFQ database migration has been applied before using the module.
  • Test the complete process with a controlled supplier email: issue an RFQ, receive the invitation, open the link, submit a test quotation and verify that staff can compare it.
  • If an invitation is not received, check the supplier email address, SMTP configuration and the error reported when the RFQ is issued.

9. Quick process summary

Staff creates RFQ → selects suppliers → issues invitations → supplier receives email → supplier opens secure link → supplier submits quotation online → staff compares quotations → staff awards quotation → staff creates purchase order.
